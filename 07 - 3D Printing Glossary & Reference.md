# 📖 3D Printing Glossary & Reference

A quick-reference dictionary of common 3D printing concepts, slicer parameters, materials science terms, and hardware components.

---

## 🔤 A – Z Glossary

### A
* **AMS (Automatic Material System):** Bambu Lab's multi-spool automated feeder that switches filaments mid-print, provides dry storage, and enables multi-color or multi-material printing.
* **Auxiliary Nozzle:** On dual-extrusion printers like the **Bambu X2D**, the second independent nozzle dedicated to extruding non-bonding support interface materials (e.g., Support for ABS) without purging.

### B
* **Bed Leveling (Auto Bed Leveling / ABL):** The automated process where the printer probes multiple points across the build surface with a strain gauge or eddy-current sensor to create a mesh map of surface imperfections, adjusting the Z-axis in real time to ensure a flawless first layer.
* **Bowden Extruder:** An extrusion system where the motor is mounted to the printer frame and pushes filament through a long Teflon (PTFE) tube to the hotend. *(Contrast with Direct Drive).*
* **Bridging:** Printing a horizontal span of filament in mid-air between two raised points without supports beneath it. Requires strong part cooling and tuned bridging speed.
* **Brim:** A single-layer wide flat flange printed around the perimeter of a part to increase bed contact area and prevent corner lifting (warping).

### C
* **Carbon Fiber (CF) Filled Filament:** Standard polymers (PLA, PETG, Nylon) blended with micro-chopped carbon fibers. Greatly increases stiffness, dimensional stability, and temperature tolerance, while giving a matte textured finish. Requires a hardened steel nozzle (0.6mm recommended).
* **Chamber Temperature:** The ambient air temperature inside the printer's enclosed volume. High-performance engineering plastics (ASA, PC-ABS, Nylon) require actively heated chambers (55–65°C+) to eliminate internal stress and delamination.
* **CoreXY:** A high-speed kinematic motion system using two stationary stepper motors and crossed timing belts to move the lightweight print head in both X and Y directions simultaneously. Enables extreme acceleration with minimal moving mass.
* **Creep (Thermal / Mechanical):** The tendency of a solid plastic to slowly deform permanently under continuous mechanical load or heat. PLA creeps easily under tool weight in warm environments; ASA and PC-ABS resist creep.

### D
* **Delamination:** When individual printed layers fail to fuse together and split or crack apart horizontally under load or temperature shock. Common in unheated ABS/ASA prints.
* **Direct Drive:** An extrusion system where the drive gears and stepper motor are mounted directly on the toolhead right above the hotend. Delivers precise retraction and effortlessly handles flexible filaments (TPU).

### F
* **Flow Dynamics / Pressure Advance (K-Factor):** An advanced slicer algorithm that compensates for the elastic pressure buildup of molten plastic inside the nozzle during acceleration and deceleration. Prevents bulging corners and under-extruded seam lines.
* **Flow Rate (Extrusion Multiplier):** A calibration factor that determines the volume of plastic extruded per millimeter of movement. Typically tuned around 0.95 to 1.02.

### G
* **G-code:** The numerical machine control language generated by a slicer that instructs 3D printer motors where to move, how fast to accelerate, and what temperature to maintain.
* **Glass Transition Temperature ($T_g$):** The critical temperature threshold where an amorphous plastic transitions from a hard, glassy state to a soft, rubbery state. For PLA, $T_g$ is ~55°C; for ASA, it is ~100°C.

### H
* **Hardened Steel Nozzle:** A durable wear-resistant nozzle required when printing abrasive filaments like carbon fiber, glass fiber, or glow-in-the-dark plastics (which quickly erode standard brass nozzles).
* **Heat Deflection Temperature (HDT):** The temperature at which a standard test bar bends a specified distance under a specified mechanical load. Indicates real-world temperature limits.
* **Hygroscopic:** The tendency of a material to actively absorb water molecules directly from atmospheric humidity. Nylons, PC, and PETG are highly hygroscopic and must be dried before printing.

### I
* **Infill:** The internal lattice structure printed inside the walls of a solid 3D part to support top layers and provide rigidity. Common patterns include Gyroid, Adaptive Cubic, and Honeycomb.
* **Input Shaping / Resonance Compensation:** An accelerometer-based algorithm that measures the natural harmonic vibration of the printer frame and counteracts vibration waves in real time, eliminating surface ghosting and ringing at high speeds.
* **Ironing:** A slicer feature where the heated nozzle makes a slow, low-extrusion second pass directly over the topmost flat layer, smoothing out layer ridges like a clothes iron for an injection-molded look.

### K
* **K-Factor:** See *Pressure Advance*.

### O
* **Overhang Angle:** The degree of slant of an outward-sloping surface relative to vertical ($0^\circ$). Overhangs up to $45^\circ$ typically print cleanly without support; angles steeper than $55^\circ$ require support structures.

### P
* **PEI (Polyetherimide):** The gold standard coating for spring-steel build plates. Textured PEI offers incredible adhesion when hot and automatically releases prints when cooled to room temperature.
* **Poop Chute:** The waste purge chute at the rear of Bambu printers where purged filament beads are ejected during tool changes, nozzle cleaning, or startup routines.
* **Purge Tower / Prime Tower:** A small sacrificial rectangular tower printed alongside multi-material prints to re-pressurize the nozzle and clean any residue before resuming the main model.

### R
* **Raft:** A thick sacrificial base layer printed onto the build plate before the model starts. Used primarily for parts with tiny footprints or on uneven surfaces.
* **Retraction:** Quickly pulling a fraction of a millimeter of filament back into the hotend before moving across empty space to prevent oozing and stringing.

### S
* **Seam (Z-Seam):** The tiny visual mark or line left on the outer surface of a print where the nozzle starts and stops each individual layer loop. Slicers allow aligning seams to rear corners.
* **Stringing:** Hair-like wisps of plastic left between separated sections of a print. Caused by wet filament, high nozzle temperatures, or insufficient retraction distance.

### T
* **Toolpath:** The exact calculated trajectory that the nozzle follows while laying down plastic for perimeters, infill, and solid surfaces.
* **Tree Supports:** Organic, branch-like support columns that snake upward from the build plate around a model. Faster to print, uses less plastic, and is easier to detach than traditional grid supports.

### W
* **Walls / Perimeters:** The solid concentric outer boundary loops of a printed part. Adding walls increases strength faster than increasing infill density.
* **Warping:** When uneven thermal cooling causes the corners of a printed part to shrink and pull upward away from the build plate. Prevented by using brims, clean PEI plates, and actively heated enclosures.

### Z
* **Z-Banding:** Repeating horizontal banding or uneven line texture across the height of a print, usually caused by lead screw wobble, belt tension issues, or cyclic bed temperature fluctuations.
* **Zero-Gap Support:** Printing a support interface layer directly touching the model with zero clearance ($0.0\text{ mm}$ Z-distance) using two chemically incompatible materials that pop apart effortlessly without welding.
